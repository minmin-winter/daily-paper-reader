## ABSTRACT 

Multimodal reasoning requires models to think with different modalities and in-tegrate their complementary information throughout the reasoning process. How-ever, existing methods often concatenate modality-specific reasoning steps into a single sequence, creating a modality-switching gap whenever reasoning moves from one modality to another. We introduce Uni-LaDiR ( Uni fied La tent Di ffusion 

Reasoner), a framework that brings these multimodal reasoning steps into a uni-fied latent space. A unified encoder maps teacher reasoning steps from different modalities into latent thought tokens in a shared space, trained to preserve the information needed for later reasoning steps and the final output. Because the same context can support multiple valid next steps, we use diffusion to predict the next block of thought tokens from the input and preceding blocks. Jointly training the encoder and diffusion reasoner with shared model weights encour-ages thought tokens to be both useful for the task and predictable from the avail-able context. At inference, the model generates these tokens without teacher ob-servations. Across eleven vision-language model (VLM) benchmarks and two vision-language-action (VLA) suites, Uni-LaDiR achieves relative gains over the strongest baselines of 7.3% on four mathematical and logical VLM benchmarks and 6.1% on RLBench manipulation tasks. 

## 1 INTRODUCTION 

A world can be observed in different modalities. An image shows how it looks like, language describes what is happening, and 3D point clouds reveal the geometry and robot states represent the interaction. These modalities provide complementary evidence. Many multimodal problems can only be solved by combining them (Lu et al., 2022b; 2024; Hao et al., 2025) and a capable multimodal reasoner should therefore be able to use information from different modalities in its reasoning process (Gao et al., 2024; Gu et al., 2025). Recent work has explored several ways to combine multimodal information in Chain-of-Thought reasoning. For vision-language models (VLMs), prior work interleaves text with visual reason-ing steps, where the visual content takes the form of generated images (Li et al., 2025c; Gu et al., 2025) or latent visual tokens (Li et al., 2025b; Yang et al., 2025). For vision-language-action mod-els (VLAs), prior work combines language, visual inputs, and actions (Brohan et al., 2023; Kim et al., 2024; Zhao et al., 2025), with some methods also incorporating 3D information and robot states (Zhen et al., 2024; Qu et al., 2025; Liu et al., 2026). Despite their differences, most methods follow a similar pattern: each modality is encoded into its own tokens or features, which are then concatenated or interleaved in the LLM sequence (Gao et al., 2024; Li et al., 2025b; Yang et al., 2025; Liu et al., 2026). As a result, reasoning still happens over modality-specific representations. When successive reasoning steps use different modalities, the model needs to transition both the reasoning state and the representation space, where switching representation spaces is not part of the reasoning itself and adds unnecessary complexity to the reasoning process. To address this issue, we decouple the reasoning-state transition from representation-space tran-sition by modeling the multimodal reasoning steps into a unified latent space . Once this information is encoded into a shared latent space, the reasoning state can evolve independently of the observation modality. This design gives subsequent reasoning a shared representation space instead of requiring modality-specific latent representation (Kaelbling et al., 1998; Hafner et al., 2019; 2020). Based on this idea, we introduce Uni-LaDiR, a unified latent diffusion framework for multimodal reasoning. Unlike prior approaches that directly concatenate outputs from modality-specific en-coders, Uni-LaDiR introduces a unified encoder that maps heterogeneous modality features into a 

shared latent reasoning space . Specifically, every teacher reasoning step is encoded into a fixed-width latent block as thought tokens, and each block is trained to support the generation of future reasoning steps and the final task output, rather than to reconstruct its source modality. This encour-ages the thought tokens to preserve information according to its utility for subsequent reasoning, while factoring out modality-specific representational differences that are irrelevant to the task. However, learning to predict these thought tokens is not trivial. First, the next reasoning step is often not unique: the same context may support multiple valid intermediate thoughts or actions. We therefore use diffusion to model the conditional distribution of the next thought tokens given the input and preceding thought tokens (Ho et al., 2020; Rombach et al., 2022; Chi et al., 2023; Kang et al., 2025b; 2026c). Second, useful thought tokens should satisfy two properties: reasoning sufficiency and context predictability . They should preserve the information needed for subsequent reasoning and the final answer, while remaining predictable from the available context at inference time. We therefore jointly train the unified encoder and the latent diffusion reasoner, allowing the representation space and the reasoning model to adapt to each other. This encourages thought tokens that retain task-relevant information and can be generated from the available context. We evaluate Uni-LaDiR on eleven VLM reasoning benchmarks and two VLA manipulation suites. Compared with the strongest reported modality-specific latent reasoning baseline in each setting, 2Preprint. Uni-LaDiR improves mean accuracy by 7.3% relative across four mathematical and logical VLM benchmarks and mean success by 6.1% relative across ten RLBench manipulation tasks. Joint training. A useful thought token needs to retain task-relevant information and be predictable from the context available at inference. To meet both requirements, we share the LLM weights between the unified encoder and diffusion reasoner (Duggal et al., 2026) and optimize 

L = Lcont + λLdiff , (5) where λ balances the two losses. The continuation loss trains the encoder to retain information needed for later reasoning and the final output. The diffusion loss trains the shared backbone to predict these representations from context, so its updates also change how the encoder represents teacher steps. Although we stop diffusion gradients through the clean targets and conditioning prefix, both losses still update the shared LLM weights. Diffusion training therefore also updates the unified encoder, allowing task utility and context predictability to jointly shape the latent representations. 

Inference. At inference, the teacher reasoning steps and unified encoding path are not required. Starting from x, the model generates z1, . . . , z K sequentially by denoising one latent block at a time, with each block conditioned on the previously generated blocks; K is not fixed, and generation stops once the model stops predicting <BOT> (Appendix B.2) (Figure 2, bottom). The complete latent sequence then conditions prediction of the final answer or action y.

VLM visual reasoning. On the seven vision-centric benchmarks, Uni-LaDiR improves average accuracy over Qwen2.5-VL-7B by 21.4%; on the four mathematical and logical benchmarks, the relative gain is 34.7% (Table 1). It also outperforms the strongest latent baseline in each group: 4.7% over Mull-Tokens and 7.3% over ILVR on average. The gains over modality-specific base-lines reach 30.3% on VisualPuzzles over Mirage, and 10.8% on MathVision and 12.6% on Visu-Logic over ILVR. The advantage over modality-specific baselines suggests that sharing reasoning representations benefits both visual reasoning and mathematical tasks. 

VLA manipulation. Uni-LaDiR improves average success over the strongest prior latent policies by 0.45 percentage points over PearlVLA + PRL on LIBERO and 6.1% relative to LaST 0 on RL-Bench (Tables 3 and 2). On RLBench, it improves nine of ten tasks and matches the remaining one; the largest gain is 16.7% on Plants. These reported means place Uni-LaDiR among the strongest policies in this comparison. Without action chunking, Uni-LaDiR runs at 16.5 Hz , a 7.1% higher inference rate than LaST 0 and 15.0 × the rate of explicit CoT-VLA. 4.3 ANALYSIS

## 5 RELATED WORK 

Unified multimodal models. Early generalist models cast heterogeneous tasks as a shared se-quence problem, spanning vision–language understanding, generation, and embodied control (Wang et al., 2022; Lu et al., 2022a; 2023; Reed et al., 2022; Mizrahi et al., 2023; Bachmann et al., 2024), and large-scale vision–language models connected pretrained language models to visual inputs through learned interfaces (Alayrac et al., 2022; Chen et al., 2022; 2023; Huang et al., 2023; Peng et al., 2023). Autoregressive any-to-any systems represent multiple modalities in a common token stream (Sun et al., 2023b;a; Yu et al., 2023; Ge et al., 2023; Jin et al., 2023; Wu et al., 2023b; Zhan et al., 2024; Chameleon Team, 2024; Wang et al., 2024c), while hybrid autoregressive–diffusion ar-chitectures unify discrete and continuous generation (Tang et al., 2023; Zhou et al., 2024a; Xie et al., 2024; Sun et al., 2024; Wu et al., 2024; Chen et al., 2025b; Deng et al., 2025a; Xu et al., 2025a; Bai et al., 2025). Whereas these models unify what is read and generated, Uni-LaDiR unifies the rea-soning in between, representing and predicting the intermediate thoughts from every modality in a single latent space. 

Multimodal reasoning. Multimodal chain-of-thought methods supervise or elicit textual ratio-nales from visual evidence (Kang et al., 2025a; Lu et al., 2022b; Zhang et al., 2024; Rose et al., 2023; Xu et al., 2024; Dong et al., 2024; Guo et al., 2024; Yao et al., 2024; Huang et al., 2025b), or externalize computation through programs, tools, image operations, spatial abstractions, and visual scratchpads (Gupta & Kembhavi, 2022; Suris et al., 2023; Yang et al., 2023b; Wu et al., 2023a; Hu et al., 2023; Chen et al., 2024; Hu et al., 2024; Zhou et al., 2024b). Recent work interleaves language with visual thoughts (Gao et al., 2024; Li et al., 2025a; Gu et al., 2025; Li et al., 2026b), and embodied methods guide control with language plans, visual predictions, and state or action trajectories (Ahn et al., 2022; Huang et al., 2022; Driess et al., 2023; Brohan et al., 2023; Jiang et al., 2022; Li et al., 2023b; Kim et al., 2024; Li et al., 2024; Zhao et al., 2025; Zhong et al., 2026; Bai et al., 2026; Liu et al., 2026). These methods reason by switching between modality-specific representations, such as textual rationales and generated images, whereas Uni-LaDiR reasons in one shared space into which text, images, 3D structure, and robot state are all encoded. 

Latent reasoning. Latent methods internalize rationales or allocate extra computation without decoding every step (Zelikman et al., 2022; Goyal et al., 2023; Deng et al., 2023; 2024; Pfau et al., 2024; Zelikman et al., 2024; Yu et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2024a), compute through recurrence or continuous semantic states (Dehghani et al., 2018; Giannou et al., 2023; Yang et al., 2023a; Kohli et al., 2026; Jeddi et al., 2026; Yang et al., 2026; Tu et al., 2026), or replace language rationales with continuous thoughts (Hao et al., 2024; Shen et al., 2025; Xu et al., 2025c;d; Deng et al., 2025b). Multimodal variants build latent thoughts from visual features, perceptual teachers, helper images, or task loss (Kang et al., 2026a; Pham & Ngo, 2025; Li et al., 2025b; Yang et al., 2025; Qin et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2026; Jeon et al., 2026; Viveiros et al., 2026a; Li et al., 2026a; Xu et al., 2026a; Ray et al., 2025; Hu et al., 2026; Chen et al., 2026a; Shao et al., 2026; Chen et al., 2026b), and diffusion-based approaches model multimodal or multi-step latent trajectories (Kang et al., 2025b; Tu et al., 2026; He et al., 2023; Huang et al., 2025a; Bai et al., 2026; Liu et al., 2026; Viveiros et al., 2026b; Wu et al., 2026; Fan et al., 2026; Kang et al., 2026c). Mull-Tokens (Ray et al., 2025) comes closest: its latent tokens are shared across modalities, but each is still pulled toward the target of its own step, either text tokens or frozen image-encoder features, and this step-level supervision is dropped in later stages. LaST 0 (Liu et al., 2026) is the closest VLA counterpart but uses a separate latent format for each modality. Unlike prior methods, which keep modality-specific latent formats or force each latent to match a text or image representation, Uni-LaDiR learns the latent space itself: one encoder maps text, images, 3D structure, and robot state into shared thought tokens, which are trained by the steps that follow and generated by diffusion. 

## 6 CONCLUSION 

We introduce Uni-LaDiR, a unified latent diffusion framework for multimodal reasoning. A shared encoder maps teacher steps from different modalities into thought tokens, and a jointly trained dif-fusion reasoner predicts these tokens under downstream reasoning and task supervision. This gives heterogeneous intermediate steps a unified latent space, allowing the model to reason without gener-ating modality-specific representations or accessing teacher observations at inference. B.2 TRAINING AND INFERENCE DETAILS 

VLM training. We train on Zebra-CoT (Li et al., 2025a), using its interleaved text and image steps as teacher supervision. We use AdamW with a peak learning rate of 10 −5, cosine decay, a global batch size of 64, and one epoch of training, informed by prior multimodal fine-tuning recipes (Li et al., 2025a; Hu et al., 2026). We use bf16, a maximum sequence length of 8,192, 3% warmup, weight decay of 0.01, and gradient clipping at 1.0. Each backbone retains its native image processor. The unified encoder and shared reasoning backbone are optimized jointly; teacher traces are omitted at evaluation. 

Thought tokens and latent sampling. We use four 512-dimensional thought tokens per block, with learned projections to and from the backbone width, taking the token budget from a latent-diffusion reference configuration (Kang et al., 2025b). The loss weights are λy = 2 and λ = 5 .Training retains the annotated reasoning-step sequence, and the number of thought blocks is not fixed. At inference, the model decides when to stop: after each block, it predicts the next token, and if this is the special token <BOT> , it generates another block; otherwise, the latent chain ends. For VLMs, the chain is followed by greedy answer decoding with a 512-token limit. For flow matching, we draw ϵi ∼ N (0 , I ) for each block and t ∼ U [0 , 1] , set zti = (1 − t)ϵi +

t sg( z⋆i ), and use v⋆i = z⋆i − ϵi as the velocity target (Lipman et al., 2023). At inference, we integrate from t = 0 to 1 per block with one sampled trajectory and no classifier-free guidance. Table 5 collects these settings; VLM and VLA training use the same loss weights. 

RLBench. We evaluate ten tasks with a Franka Panda in CoppeliaSim, using 100 waypoint-generated training demonstrations per task and keyframe sampling (Liu et al., 2026). Inputs include a front-view RGB image resized to 384 × 384 , a point cloud sampled to 1,024 points, the task in-struction, and proprioception. We evaluate the final checkpoint with 20 rollouts per task and seed, giving 600 rollouts across three seeds. 

LIBERO. We train a separate policy for each of the four suites, each containing ten tasks and 500 training demonstrations. Inputs use third-person and wrist RGB views resized to 384 × 384 . We omit the point-cloud teacher because this modality is unavailable, and sample intermediate teacher steps every eight frames (Liu et al., 2026). Latent target supervision. In Figure 4, reconstruction predicts the source teacher features from each thought block; continuation prediction predicts later teacher steps. Both variants retain the same final-output loss λy Lout and propagate its gradients through the thought tokens into the unified encoder and shared backbone. Text CE is averaged over supervised tokens, and continuous L2 loss 20 Preprint. is averaged over target elements, using the same reduction and loss weights in both variants. Teacher targets remain fixed. Thus, the comparison changes intermediate supervision while preserving the encoder’s access to final-task supervision. 

Thought-token prediction. In Figure 6, all three objectives are trained jointly with the unified en-coder, using the same continuation loss and loss weights; only the thought-token prediction objective changes. L2 and cosine regression directly predict the next clean thought block. Flow matching pre-dicts the velocity along the noisy-to-clean path, using the sampler specified in Appendix B.1. Each method conditions on the same input and preceding thought blocks and supplies its predicted tokens to the downstream reasoner. ## C LIMITATIONS 

Our experiments focus on whether reasoning in a shared latent space helps, and we leave a closer study of the teacher CoTs themselves to future work. Beyond which modalities are shared (Sec-tion 4.3), we do not analyze how the teacher CoTs affect the learned reasoning, for example the granularity of teacher steps or the source and quality of the traces. In our setting, the VLA teachers are future images, point clouds, and robot states taken from the demonstrations, and the VLM teach-ers are the interleaved traces in Zebra-CoT; other sources, such as tactile signals or model-generated traces, may shape the latent space differently. 22
