Abstract 

Recent advances in vision-language modeling have substantially improved multimodal en-coding, retrieval and reasoning. Yet for mul-timodal recommendation, encoding rich item vision-language semantic interactions remains a long-standing bottleneck, which hampers ac-curate item representation learning and user-item matching. Mainstream approaches pri-marily adopt independent encoding of vision and language modality followed by rigid late fusion such as concatenation, inherently omit-ting native vision-language interactions and introducing cross-modal semantic distortion. To address this challenge, we propose Opti-calRec, the first visual-space unified encoding paradigm for multimodal collaborative filter-ing, a fundamental recommendation setting. In-stead of isolated modality-specific encoding, OpticalRec renders item textual metadata as visual glyphs, enabling native image–text inter-action within visual encoder—the perceptual encoding level. The resulting representations are further processed by the language decoder— the semantic encoding level, allowing Optical-Rec to exploit the dual-attention mechanism of modern vision-language models that previ-ous encoding methods omitted. OpticalRec’s efficacy is theoretically supported by mutual information analysis and empirically demon-strated through superior performance across strong baselines and benchmarks. As a plug-and-play module, OpticalRec (1) introduces minimal cost, (2) is robust against rendered text font, color and layout, etc., and (3) integrates seamlessly into existing multimodal collabora-tive filtering models. 

1 Introduction 

Recent progress in vision-language modeling and multimodal learning has opened new opportunities for recommender systems (OpenAI, 2026; Li et al., 2024a; Bai et al., 2025; Xing et al., 2025; Kim et al.,

2026). These improvements mainly stem from two aspects: First, textual and visual representations of-fer rich semantic signals that help characterize user interests and item properties. Second, the above representations learned during large-scale vision-language pretraining are transferrable to recommen-dation tasks (Malitesta et al., 2025; Xu et al., 2025). As a result, large vision-language model representa-tions have become a powerful basis for improving recommendation quality, contextual understanding, and personalization in modern recommender sys-tems (Pomo et al., 2025; Ye et al., 2025). Despite above mentioned benefits of large vision-language models, injecting rich multimodal fea-tures into recommender systems continues to pose significant challenges. A fundamental bottleneck lies in the widely-adopted decoupled/independent encoding of each modality followed by the lossy late fusion (Xu et al., 2025; Malitesta et al., 2025). Two major disadvantages exist here: 1. indepen-dent encoding inherently omits vision-language cross-interactions such as cross attention and yields embeddings in heterogeneous vector space per-modality, 2. the lossy late fusion such as sum-mation, concatenation and graph fusion, is ap-plied to integrate the heterogeneous embedding in 1., which distorts the cross-modal semantics in resulting encodings. Specifically, summation or concatenation-based late fusion omits vision-language cross interactions with fusion-entailed information loss (Zhou et al., 2025a); Graph-based methods exploit multimodal fusion by similarity graphs, but initial graph construction still falls back to late-fused separate encoding (Zhang et al., 2021; Zhou and Shen, 2023). Recent works continue to refine downstream graph architectures while over-looking image-text interactions and the disruptive late fusion (Ong and Khong, 2025; Yu et al., 2025). Hence, we analyze that a unified-space encoding paradigm is optimal for multimodal recommenda-tion that satisfies: 1arXiv:2610.05432v1 [cs.IR] 4 Oct 2026 1. Multimodal content should be presented in a unified input space, allowing rich vision-language interactions instead of isolation. 2. The unified input should be encoded once into a homogeneous embedding space, without ad-ditional disruptive late fusion. Motivated by these observations (Bai et al., 2025; OpenGV, 2025), we propose OpticalRec, a lightweight, plug-and-play unified encoding frame-work for multimodal recommendation. Through an efficient, one-time rendering operation that transforms textual metadata into visual text/glyph, OpticalRec enables rich images and text cross-interactions within a unified pixel space during vision encoding. This also naturally aligns with a “see-as-users-see” principle: users shift atten-tion back-and-forth between item image and text when browsing online shopping content. Further processed by the language decoder, OpticalRec together forms a dual attention mechanism and out-puts homogeneous embeddings without late fusion. We summarize our contribution as follows: 1. We propose OpticalRec, the first visual-space unified encoding framework for multimodal collaborative filtering allowing rich, dual at-tention -based image-text cross-interactions. 2. OpticalRec preserves vision-language correla-tions without the widely adopted, lossy late fu-sion, and is supported by theoretical analysis via mutual information and matrix effective rank. 3. Experiments across multiple Amazon Review benchmarks and recommendation backbones demonstrate OpticalRec’s consistent improve-ments over strong multimodal baseline en-coders. 4. OpticalRec is lightweight, plug-and-play, and robust against input layout, text color, font etc., making it readily adaptable to exist-ing multimodal collaborative filtering frame-works. 

2 Related Works 

2.1 Multimodal Collaborative Filtering 

Pretrained language and vision-language models are widely adopted in multimodal collaborative filtering (MMCF) for transfer learning (Malitesta et al., 2024; Xu et al., 2025). A major line of work adopts pretrained multimodal embeddings as frozen item features for collaborative filtering (Xin et al., 2019; Wang et al., 2021; Zhou et al., 2025b; Li et al., 2024c; Sheng et al., 2024). Early meth-ods such as VBPR (He and McAuley, 2016) lever-age CNN-based image features to model visually driven user preferences. Later methods further introduce textual features and graph neural net-works for high-order multimodal interaction model-ing (Chen et al., 2017; Zhou, 2023; Yu et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2023; Zhou et al., 2023a). For exam-ple, MMGCN (Wei et al., 2019) and GRCN (Wei et al., 2020) extend user-item graph with modality-aware fusion. Later developments such as LAT-TICE (Zhang et al., 2021) and FREEDOM (Zhou and Shen, 2023) introduce item-item content sim-ilarity graphs for augmentation. Recent methods continue to explore tailored graph structures and better training supervision (Zheng et al., 2024; Xu et al., 2025; Liu et al., 2025; Guo et al., 2024; Zhou et al., 2023b). In contrast, our work shifts the fo-cus from increasingly specialized graph networks to unified encoding for more advanced image-text interactions. Beyond feature-based MMCF, recent and past studies also explore multimodal sequen-tial/generative recommendation and model behav-ior study (Wang et al., 2024; Yue et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2025b,a; Yue et al., 2025; Zhai et al., 2025; Lei et al., 2026; Qiao et al., 2026). We focus on MMCF for a cleaner encoding setup, where item representations directly interact with collaborative signals. 

2.2 Vision-Language Grounding and Unified Encoding 

Feature-based multimodal recommendation pre-dominantly follows a separate-encoding paradigm plus late fusion (Ye et al., 2025; He et al., 2025). This paradigm aligns with early vision-language representation learning such as CLIP, which learns shared image-text embeddings but still encodes each modality separately. Later models such as Flamingo and LLaVA move toward joint, inter-leaved vision-language modeling. More recent models such as Qwen-VL (Bai et al., 2025), In-ternVL (OpenGV, 2025), and LLaVA model se-ries (Li et al., 2024a,b) further strengthen visual grounding and layout-aware understanding. How-ever, how such unified vision-language perception can benefit MMCF remains unexplored, as user-2item collaborative signal is inherently multifaceted. We aim to address this challenge in the following of this work.

4 Experimental Setup 

4.1 Datasets 

For evaluation, we select three most commonly used MMCF benchmarks from Amazon.com known for real-world sparsity, namely Baby Products 

(Baby), Pet Supplies (Pets), and Clothing, Shoes & Jewelry (Clothing) (Hou et al., 2024). For pre-processing, apply 5-core and absent feature filter-ing detailed in Appendix B.1 due to space limit. Dataset statistics are reported in Table 1. 

4.2 Baseline Methods 

We compare five representative multimodal recom-mendation baselines. VBPR (He and McAuley, 2016) incorporates CNN-based visual features into BPR, while LATTICE (Zhang et al., 2021) learns modality-specific item graphs and FREE-DOM (Zhou and Shen, 2023) improves graph ro-bustness through graph freezing and denoising. 

BM3 (Zhou et al., 2023b) adopts negative-free self-supervised learning, whereas LGMRec (Guo et al., 2024) models local and global multimodal correla-tions with hypergraphs. We additionally construct a strong prompt-based VLM baseline (Figure 1(b)) to isolate OpticalRec’s perception-level interaction beyond decoder-level semantic interaction. Details are explained in Appendix B.2. 

4.3 Implementations 

We use Qwen3-VL-8B for the main experiments and keep all VLMs frozen during encoding for efficiency and fair comparison. All recommenda-tion models are trained with AdamW and early stopping, with key hyperparameters tuned on the validation set; performance is evaluated using NDCG@20, Recall@20, and Hit Ratio@20. De-tails can be viewed in Appendix B.3. 

5 Experimental Results 

5.1 Main Performance Analysis 

We report in Table 2 experimental results of inde-pendent encoding + late fusion, prompt-based en-coding, and OpticalRec encoding. Sum and Concat are standard late fusion methods; Graph denotes the graph neural network fusion module in their respec-tive recommendation models, we refer readers to Section 4.2 for original papers of modeling details. OpticalRec consistently outperforms the strongest baseline across all backbones and datasets. (1) It yields an average gain of 2.12% across all mod-els, metrics, and datasets, with the largest improve-ments on Pets (3.21% on average; up to 5.82% on NDCG@20 with FREEDOM), while gains on Baby and Clothing remain consistent at 1.65% and 1.51%. (2) Improvements are larger on NDCG@20 than Recall@20 (2.41% vs. 1.97%), indicating bet-ter top-ranked ordering in addition to stronger re-trieval. (3) Graph-based backbones benefit more than VBPR, with average gains of 2.29%, 2.88%, and 2.29% for LATTICE, FREEDOM, and LGM-Rec, respectively, versus 1.09% for VBPR, sug-gesting that richer multimodal item representations further enhance semantic propagation over inter-action graphs. To highlight the plug-and-play at-tribute of OpticalRec, we also report results using InternVL3.5-8B as the encoding vision-language model, another performant VLM in Appendix C.1.

6.2 Rendering Layout, Font Size and Color 

The item features we used for creating item cards are {title, brand, category, price}, which are natu-rally placed one-by-one below the item image as appeared similar on the amazon website. This fol-lows most natural human visual perception without cherry picking order/coordinates. Additionally, we conducted a layout robustness study where we (1) randomly shuffle the position of {title, brand cate-gory and price} for 50% of the data, (2) scale the font size by ±25%, for 50% data, and (3) change all rendered text color to black-and-white. The results are shown in Table 3. We observe that Optical-Rec is robust against layout, font size and color by performing within ±1% for all controlled fac-tors. We will also release the rendered cards and embeddings in the final version of the paper.

7 Conclusions 

We present OpticalRec, a unified optical vision-language encoding framework for multimodal col-laborative filtering. By rendering item text and images into a shared visual space, OpticalRec en-ables complementary perceptual- and semantic-level cross-modal interactions while avoiding dis-ruptive late fusion. Extensive experiments demon-8strate consistent gains across datasets and recom-mendation backbones, together with improved em-bedding quality, robustness to rendering variations, and practical one-time preprocessing cost. These results highlight unified optical encoding as a sim-ple and effective alternative to conventional inde-pendently encoded multimodal representations. 

Limitations 

This work studies unified optical encoding under feature-based multimodal collaborative filtering, which is a mainstream setting for large-scale item encoding. Although we evaluate multiple back-bones, rendering settings, text lengths, and VLM scales, broader validation across additional recom-mendation domains such as video recommendation remains valuable for future research.

B Dataset, Baseline, and Implementation Details 

B.1 Dataset Details 

For evaluating our models and fair comparison, we select three most commonly used benchmarks by multimodal collaborative filtering recommen-dation from Amazon.com known for real-world sparsity, namely Baby Products (Baby), Pet Sup-plies (Pets), and Clothing, Shoes & Jewelry (Cloth-ing) (Hou et al., 2024). For preprocessing, we follow (Malitesta et al., 2025; Pomo et al., 2025) to apply 5-core filtering to exclude users and items with less than five-time appearances. For textural feature selection, we choose title , price , brand and 

categories ; For visual features, we use photos of the items. We also filter out items without above metadata. Detailed statistics of the datasets are re-ported in Table 1 including users (#User), items (#Item), interactions (#Inter.) and dataset sparsity in percentages. 

B.2 Baseline Methods  

> •

VBPR (He and McAuley, 2016) is an early multi-modal collaborative filtering method that extends BPR with CNN-based visual features to model visually driven user preferences.  

> •

LATTICE (Zhang et al., 2021) constructs modality-aware item-item similarity graphs for each modality and applies graph convolutions to inject high-order item affinities into item repre-sentations for downstream collaborative filtering.  

> •

FREEDOM (Zhou and Shen, 2023) freezes the item-item graph and applies degree-sensitive edge pruning to denoise the user-item interaction graph, improving robustness over LATTICE’s dynamic graph learning.  

> •

BM3 (Zhou et al., 2023b) proposes a self-supervised multimodal framework that uses dropout-based contrastive views, eliminating the need for negative sampling and auxiliary graph augmentations.  

> •

LGMRec (Guo et al., 2024) captures both local and global modality correlations via hypergraph structures, modeling fine-grained cross-modal in-teractions for multimodal preference alignment. We additionally construct a strong prompt-based VLM baseline (Figure 1(b)) as a controlled com-parison, isolating the benefit of OpticalRec’s ad-ditional perception-level image–glyph interac-tion beyond decoder-level semantic interaction.
