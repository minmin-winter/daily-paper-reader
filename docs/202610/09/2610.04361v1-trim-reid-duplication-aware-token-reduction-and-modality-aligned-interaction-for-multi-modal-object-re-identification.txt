ABSTRACT 

Multi-modal object re-identification exploits complementary RGB, near-infrared (NIR), and thermal-infrared (TIR) obser-vations to retrieve target objects. However, existing meth-ods commonly employ visual encoders optimized for global image-text alignment and select tokens using learned impor-tance scores. Such designs fail to preserve fine-grained iden-tity cues or explicitly account for token redundancy, result-ing in underrepresented local evidence and duplicated tokens that lead to noisy and costly cross-modal interaction. To ad-dress this gap, we propose TRIM-ReID , a compact frame-work that unifies dense feature extraction, intra-modal token reduction, and inter-modal aligned interaction. Specifically, semantically rich and spatially coherent patch features are extracted by Dense Identity Representation (DIR) , which leverages DINOv3 to preserve fine-grained identity informa-tion. We then introduce Token Diversity Mining (TDM) to identify complementary local evidence and construct compact modality-specific token sets by suppressing repetitive patches while preserving informative diversity. Retained tokens are subsequently fused by Modal Relational Interaction (MRI) 

to enable effective information exchange across modalities, while a triangular alignment loss explicitly regularizes their joint relationships to maintain cross-modal semantic consis-tency under independent token selection. Extensive experi-ments on RGBNT201, RGBNT100, and MSVR310 demon-strate that TRIM-ReID achieves state-of-the-art performance.

1. INTRODUCTION 

Multi-modal object re-identification (ReID) leverages com-plementary observations from RGB, near-infrared (NIR), and thermal-infrared (TIR) modalities to identify objects across challenging imaging conditions. Recent studies [1–11] have explored selective token modeling and multi-modal fusion to improve the efficiency and discriminability of multi-modal

ReID. However, existing methods remain limited in preserv-ing fine-grained identity evidence while maintaining compact and semantically consistent cross-modal representations. A key challenge lies in how to construct informative and compact token representations . Existing approaches often rely on visual encoders pretrained with global image-text alignment objectives [6, 10, 11], followed by token se-lection based on learned importance scores [9]. However, importance-oriented selection does not explicitly consider token redundancy, and may retain multiple highly similar patches while discarding complementary local evidence. Re-cent findings in vision-language model token pruning [12,13] further suggest that redundancy can be more critical than token importance when constructing compact representa-tions. For multi-modal ReID, fine-grained identity cues are often distributed across local regions. Thus, token selection should not merely identify important patches, but also pre-serve diverse and complementary identity evidence. Another challenge lies in how to ensure consistent cross-modal interaction after token selection . Since different modali-ties emphasize distinct visual characteristics, independently retained tokens may exhibit heterogeneous semantic dis-tributions. Direct feature fusion can facilitate information exchange, but does not necessarily guarantee semantic con-sistency across modalities. Existing methods have explored cross-modal interaction through deformable aggregation [6], mixture of experts [11], and hypergraph modeling [10]. How-ever, the semantic consistency of independently selected modality-specific tokens remains insufficiently addressed. To address these challenges, we propose TRIM-ReID ,which unifies dense feature extraction, intra-modal token re-duction, and inter-modal aligned interaction. Dense Identity Representation (DIR) leverages DINOv3 to extract spatially coherent patch features for fine-grained identity modeling. Then, Token Diversity Mining (TDM) constructs compact token sets by suppressing repetitive patches while preserving complementary local evidence. Finally, Modal Relational Interaction (MRI) uses a Transformer for cross-modal fu-sion, with a triangular alignment loss to enforce semantic consistency. 3. EXPERIMENTS 3.1. Experiment Setup Implementation Details. Input images are resized to 256 ×

128 for RGBNT201 and 128 × 256 for RGBNT100 and MSVR310, with random flipping, cropping, and erasing ap-plied for data augmentation. The batch sizes are set to 64, 64, and 128 for RGBNT201, MSVR310, and RGBNT100, re-spectively, with 8, 4, and 16 images sampled per identity. We implement TRIM-ReID in PyTorch using two NVIDIA RTX 3090 GPUs and train it with Adam for 50 epochs. The learn-ing rate is set to 5 × 10 −4 for RGBNT201 and RGBNT100, and 5 × 10 −5 for MSVR310. For TDM, we use 4 pivot tokens for RGBNT201 and RGBNT100 and 8 for MSVR310, while the number of retained tokens is fixed to 80. λ is set to 0.04. 

Evaluation Metrics. We apply the Mean Average Precision (mAP) and Rank-K (K = 1, 5, 10) matching accuracy as eval-uation metrics to align with previous studies. 

3.2. Results and Analysis Main Results. Table 1 shows that TRIM-ReID consistently achieves the best performance across all three datasets. On RGBNT201, it reaches 85.2% mAP and 88.0% R-1, gaining 2.8 and 1.2 points compared to NEXT and UGG-ReID, re-spectively. On RGBNT100, it obtains 89.2% mAP and 98.7% R-1, surpassing by 0.1 and 0.6 points compared to STMI and UGG-ReID, respectively. Notably, on MSVR310, it achieves 68.8% mAP and 83.1% R-1, exceeding by 4.0 and 4.1 points compared to STMI and NEXT, respectively. These results in-dicate that duplication-aware token reduction and modality-aligned interaction are particularly beneficial in challenging scenarios of person and vehicle re-identification.

4. CONCLUSION 

We present TRIM-ReID , a compact framework for multi-modal object ReID that addresses redundant token selection and cross-modal inconsistency. By combining DIR, TDM and MRI, TRIM-ReID preserves fine-grained identity evi-dence, reduces redundant tokens, and promotes consistent cross-modal interaction. Extensive experiments on 3 datasets demonstrate its state-of-the-art performance. 5. ACKNOWLEDGEMENT
