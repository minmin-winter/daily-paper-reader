## Abstract 

Large speech language models have demonstrated strong capa-bilities in unified cross-modal understanding and generation, yet paralinguistic cues, especially emotion, remain difficult to preserve. Existing systems typically rely on entangled acoustic represen-tations, which allow the underlying language model to depend excessively on recovered lexical content instead of grounding its behavior in acoustic-prosodic evidence. We address this limita-tion with EMODE, an emotion-aware speech language model built around Dynamic Para-Semantic Experts (DPSE) . DPSE decom-poses continuous speech features into semantic and paralinguistic pathways, routes them dynamically, and fuses them before integra-tion into the language model. To turn this structural decomposition into functional specialization, EMODE is trained with a three-stage curriculum consisting of semantic warm-up, paralinguistic activa-tion, and joint refinement, guided by Orthogonal Expert Guidance (OEG), Semantic-to-Acoustic Alignment (SAA), and Gating Diver-sity Regularization (GDR). Experiments on SER test, empathetic response evaluation, and the newly constructed bilingual MEPA benchmark show that EMODE improves the balance between lexical fidelity and emotional sensitivity, strengthens affect-grounded re-sponse generation, and exposes the value of explicit para-semantic factorization for robust cross-corpus emotion understanding.

## 2 Related Work 2.1 Speech-Language and Audio-Language Modeling 

Research on speech-conditioned language modeling has predomi-nantly evolved along two parallel trajectories. The first focuses on developing robust speech representations and tighter speech-text interfaces. Foundational works such as Whisper [38] provide robust large-scale speech encoding, while SpeechT5 [4] and SpeechGPT [52] pioneer unified speech-text pre-training and the direct inte-gration of spoken interaction into conversational language models, respectively. Collectively, these systems establish the architectural backbone for practical, speech-native interactions. The second trajectory broadens this interface to encompass gen-eral audio understanding and end-to-end multimodal dialogue. Mod-els like Qwen-Audio and Qwen2-Audio [14, 15] unify audio instruc-tion following, whereas SALMONN [41] and Pengi [16] extend ca-pabilities to generic hearing and text-centric audio task formulation. Recent “omni” systems, including GLM-4-Voice [50], Kimi-Audio [25], Phi-4-Multimodal [2], Megrez-3B-Omni [26], MiniCPM-O [32], and Qwen2.5-Omni [48], further push the boundaries of unified multimodal dialogue. 

## 2.2 Speech Emotion Recognition and Emotion-Aware Spoken Interaction 

The field of Speech Emotion Recognition (SER) provides both the foundational acoustic cues and the rigorous evaluation paradigms relevant to our objective. Extensive surveys consistently identify prosody, timing, and voice quality as the primary carriers of affec-tive information [1, 17]. Consequently, widely adopted corpora cap-ture varied communicative contexts: IEMOCAP covers dyadic inter-actions [8], MELD introduces multi-party dialogue [35], RAVDESS and SAVEE offer controlled, acted speech [24, 28], and MSP-Podcast scales to naturalistic, in-the-wild conversational audio [9]. Concur-rently, the advent of self-supervised speech encoders—including wav2vec 2.0, HuBERT, WavLM, BEATs, and emotion2vec [6, 12, 13, 23, 29]—has demonstrated that large-scale representations transfer effectively to downstream emotion understanding tasks. Recent advancements increasingly integrate SER into broader conversational frameworks. Systems such as BLSP-Emo, OpenS2S, and OSUM-EChat inject explicit emotion-aware supervision into spoken dialogue agents [19, 44, 45]. Furthermore, SECap [49] and EmotionThinker [46] move beyond closed-set classification by gen-erating descriptive captions and reasoning over emotional speech. EMODE complements these efforts. In addition to emotion-aware supervision and generation objectives, it addresses the preceding representation bottleneck by organizing semantic and paralinguis-tic evidence before integration into the language model. 

## 2.3 Expert Routing and Disentangled Speech Representations 

Sparse expert routing provides a robust mechanism for conditional computation. Sparsely gated Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) layers pio-neered the dynamic activation of specialized subnetworks based on input characteristics [40]. Subsequent innovations, such as Switch Transformers [18] and expert-choice routing [54], refined large-scale allocation and capacity balancing. While DPSE adopts this principle of conditional specialization, it diverges significantly from traditional MoE applications: it employs precisely two experts and routes continuous speech features under explicit emotion-aware supervision, prioritizing feature disentanglement over language model capacity expansion.

This architectural choice fundamentally aligns DPSE with the literature on speech disentanglement. Prior works such as AutoVC [37] factorize speaker identity from linguistic content, Triple In-formation Bottleneck models [36] decompose speech into multiple latent variables, and SpeechSplit2.0 [10] leverages unsupervised disentanglement for controllable synthesis. EMODE adapts this intuition for speech-language modeling. However, our primary ob-jective is not synthesis control; rather, we aim to construct a speech representation that independently preserves semantic fidelity and affective faithfulness, ensuring both remain highly accessible for downstream language generation tasks. 

## 3 Approach 

Developing emotion-aware spoken interaction systems necessitates speech representations capable of capturing both lexical content (what was said) and paralinguistic delivery ( how it was said). Ful-filling this dual objective is challenging when utilizing a single, entangled speech connector, particularly given the scarcity of natu-rally occurring speech-response data with explicit affective annota-tions. Building upon the teacher-guided alignment and emotion-aware continuation paradigms established in BLSP and BLSP-Emo [43, 44], EMODE leverages a frozen text teacher to supply response-side semantic targets and reformulates emotion-labeled speech into continuation-style supervision. These complementary tech-niques mitigate the data bottleneck by reducing reliance on massive datasets of directly annotated, affect-sensitive dialog pairs. Beyond this supervision paradigm, EMODE incorporates Dynamic Para-Semantic Experts (DPSE) , an architectural module that explicitly decouples semantic and paralinguistic processing pathways prior to representation fusion.

## 4 Experiments 4.1 Experimental Setup 

Training corpora. Stage I uses speech continuation data from LibriSpeech [33], Common Voice [5], GigaSpeech [11], and Wenet-Speech [51]. These corpora provide diverse speech-transcript pairs to stabilize semantic alignment prior to introducing emotion su-pervision. To overcome the scarcity of natural, affect-grounded speech-response data, Stages II and III incorporate emotion super-vision from IEMOCAP [8], MELD [35], ESD [53], CMU-MOSEI [7], MEAD [47], EARS [39], EXPRESSO [31], and MSP-Podcast [9]. All emotion datasets are mapped into a unified five-way label space: 

neutral , happy , angry , sad , and surprised .

Evaluation corpora. Our primary evaluation suite comprises two in-domain corpora (IEMOCAP and MELD) and seven zero-shot test sets: RAVDESS [28], SAVEE [24], MERBench Test1 and Test2 [27], RESD [3], AESDD [42], and CaFE [20]. This diverse selection ex-tends our evaluation beyond English to encompass Greek, Chinese, Russian, and French conditions. For spoken empathetic interac-tion, we evaluate on SpeechAlpaca using the speech-only protocol established by BLSP-Emo [44]. We also introduce MEPA, a bilin-gual synthetic benchmark built from lexically shared first-person statements and emotion-specific empathetic responses rendered by expressive speech models. Within each group, the statement remains fixed while the speaking style varies across emotions. This controlled structure reduces transcript-level shortcuts and comple-ments conventional corpora by testing whether predictions remain sensitive to acoustic-prosodic variation. 

Model and training overview. EMODE employs Whisper-base as the speech encoder, DPSE as the bridging connector, and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct as the language model backbone. The semantic branch receives phoneme-level CTC supervision, while the paralinguistic branch is guided by utterance-level emotion labels. Their fused rep-resentation is subsequently refined via routed para-semantic resid-ual fusion. Unless otherwise specified, all reported results reflect the default three-stage training curriculum detailed in Section 3: semantic warm-up, paralinguistic activation, and joint refinement.

Empathetic Response. The SpeechAlpaca benchmark evaluates a model’s ability to generate helpful and affectively appropriate responses from speech input. The primary metrics are quality , re-flecting helpfulness and coherence, and empathy , measuring how well a response acknowledges the speaker’s emotional state. Both metrics are scored on a [0, 10 ] scale by a unified DeepSeek-based evaluator across all methods. SpeechAlpaca also includes a four-way speech-only SER protocol over neutral , happy , angry , and sad ;we report this score as an auxiliary measure rather than the primary response criterion. These single-judge scores are not a substitute for human evaluation.

## 4.3 Main Results 

SER Comparison. We first ask whether explicit para-semantic factorization improves standard SER not only on familiar corpora but also under cross-corpus and cross-lingual shift. Table 1 shows that EMODE attains the highest average accuracy across the full nine-dataset suite, reaching 72.10%. The improvement is not con-centrated on a single benchmark. Instead, EMODE ranks first on MELD, RAVDESS, SAVEE, MERBench Test1, MERBench Test2, and AESDD, indicating that the same representation strategy remains effective across conversational, acted, and multilingual zero-shot settings. This comparison also clarifies the complementary strengths of the strongest baselines. EmotionThinker remains strongest on IEMOCAP, while BLSP-Emo performs best on RESD and CaFE. EMODE does not dominate every corpus individually, yet it ex-hibits the most favorable aggregate profile. This pattern matters because the central goal is not to overfit one dataset family, but to maintain emotion sensitivity without sacrificing the lexical stabil-ity required for speech-conditioned language modeling. The gains therefore support the claim that a dedicated paralinguistic pathway improves robustness when prosody, recording style, and language vary simultaneously.

## 5 Conclusion 

In this paper, we introduced EMODE , an emotion-aware speech-language model designed to address the limitations of entangled acoustic representations. Its DPSE module organizes semantic and paralinguistic processing into dedicated pathways and recombines them through residual fusion. The coordinated three-stage curricu-lum uses Orthogonal Expert Guidance (OEG), Semantic-to-Acoustic Alignment (SAA), and Gating Diversity Regularization (GDR) to establish complementary supervision, align affective context, and discourage premature routing collapse. EMODE obtains the highest average accuracy among the com-pared systems across a nine-dataset SER suite and the highest model-judged quality and empathy scores on SpeechAlpaca. Results on the controlled MEPA benchmark provide further evidence that EMODE uses acoustic-prosodic information when lexical content is held fixed. The objective and connector ablations further asso-ciate the gains with complementary expert supervision and struc-tured fusion. Together, these results support explicit para-semantic factorization for affect-sensitive spoken dialogue systems. Future work will examine human evaluation, spontaneous affective speech, synthesis-robust evaluation, and broader multilingual interactions.
