# MMVistaReason: Toward Open-Data and Post-Training Recipes for Multimodal Reasoning 

Juekai Lin ∗1,2 , Honglin Lin ∗2,3 , Yuqian Yuan 1, Xiaolong Wu 2,4 , Jie Cao 1, Liang Liang 1,

Yunqi Cao 1, Yun Zhu 1, Wenqiao Zhang 1, Lijun Wu 21Zhejiang University, 2Shanghai Artificial Intelligence Laboratory, OpenDataLab, 3Shanghai Jiao Tong University, 4Tongji University Open multimodal reasoning models have benefited from large-scale reasoning supervision, yet reliable post-training remains challenging, with bottlenecks extending beyond model scaling to uneven data quality, inefficient supervision construction, imbalanced difficulty, and cross-domain interference. We introduce MMVistaReason (MVR) , an open-data and post-training recipe that addresses these challenges from three perspectives: (1) broader capability coverage ,which organizes multimodal reasoning into two complementary groups—Analytical reasoning, emphasizing structured and knowledge-intensive problem solving, and Real-World reasoning, emphasizing visual perception and spatial grounding; (2) efficient SFT and RL data construc-tion , which standardizes heterogeneous open data through staged cleaning and annotation, combines difficulty-aware cascaded teacher distillation with answer-likelihood-based trajectory selection to construct MVR-SFT-528K, and applies scale-specific frontier filtering for MVR-RL-63K; and (3) specialize-then-integrate training , which trains complementary RL experts for each reasoning group and consolidates their capabilities through multi-teacher on-policy distillation (MOPD). Our analyses reveal a capacity-dependent interaction between supervi-sion difficulty, trajectory quality, and model capacity, showing that smaller students benefit more from selected supervision while larger students are more robust to trajectory variation and mixed-domain interference. We find that mixed-domain RL introduces benchmark-level negative transfer, whereas MOPD provides more consistent capability integration, with the pre-ferred KL direction varying across model scales. Across 15 multimodal benchmarks, MVR-4B achieves an average score of 72.8, outperforming Qwen3.5-9B (Instruct) and MMFineReason-8B while using about 70% fewer samples than MMFineReason. Scaling to 9B further improves the average to 74.4, surpassing Qwen3.5-35B-A3B (Instruct). Overall, MMVistaReason demon-strates that systematic open-data construction and capacity-aware post-training provide a practical and scalable path toward reliable multimodal reasoning models.

# 1 Introduction 

Open multimodal reasoning has rapidly progressed from visual instruction tuning to large-scale reasoning supervision. Recent long-form reasoning datasets have substantially improved mathematical and scientific reasoning in open vision-language models (VLMs) [22, 26, 75, 35, 50, 27], as well as diagrammatic and spatial reasoning [65, 2, 32, 71]. In particular, MMFineReason (MFR) shows that carefully curated supervision enables compact VLMs to compete with much larger models, while difficulty-aware subsets can approach full-data performance. Meanwhile, advances in large language 1arXiv:2610.01352v1 [cs.CV] 1 Oct 2026 MMVistaReason: Toward Open-Data and Post-Training Recipes for Multimodal Reasoning 

models (LLMs) have highlighted systematic post-training recipes, including high-quality instruction data [12, 70], rejection sampling [21, 18], verifiable feedback [5], and reinforcement learning [59]. Extending these principles to multimodal reasoning remains challenging because open multimodal data vary in visual context, quality, answerability, format, and difficulty. Therefore, reliable multimodal reasoning requires not only larger models or more data, but systematic recipes for constructing supervision, adapting it to model capacity, and integrating complementary reasoning capabilities. Despite recent progress in multimodal reasoning data construction and post-training [22, 26, 75, 65, 2], three key challenges remain: • Broader and more balanced capability coverage. Existing multimodal reasoning data are still dominated by analytical tasks such as mathematics, science, and structured visual reasoning, while real-world capabilities including perception, counting, spatial understanding, and GUI grounding receive comparatively less emphasis. A reliable multimodal reasoner should therefore develop strong and balanced capabilities across both analytical and real-world scenarios. • Data efficiency and adaptive distillation. Multimodal samples vary in difficulty and trajectory quality, while conventional pipelines often distill supervision from a single large teacher at high inference cost. Moreover, SFT and RL require different supervision: SFT benefits from reliable trajectories matched to student capacity, whereas RL favors frontier prompts that remain learnable under the current policy, motivating efficient and stage-specific data construction. • Cross-domain interference and capability integration. Training heterogeneous reasoning do-mains under a single mixed objective can introduce negative transfer across tasks [45]. While specialist models can better capture domain-specific capabilities, integrating these complemen-tary strengths into a unified generalist remains underexplored. On-policy distillation methods [1, 36] provide a promising direction for capability integration on student-generated trajectories. To address these challenges, we introduce MMVistaReason (MVR) , a systematic framework that advances existing multimodal reasoning recipes in both data construction and model optimization. We build a broader and more balanced corpus spanning two complementary groups: Analytical , covering mathematics, science, visual logic, and charts with an emphasis on structured, knowledge-intensive, and symbolic reasoning; Real-World , covering general perception, counting, spatial understanding, and GUI grounding with greater emphasis on fine-grained visual perception and spatial grounding. 

For SFT , we develop a difficulty-aware cascaded distillation pipeline that identifies challenging samples and routes unresolved examples to progressively stronger same-family teachers. Verified correct trajectories are selected through Answer-Likelihood Best-of-N, yielding reliable and difficulty-stratified supervision. This enables us to study how supervision difficulty and trajectory quality interact with model capacity. For RL , we use the rollout pass rates of the SFT model at each scale to identify frontier prompts with learning potential. We balance these prompts across the Analytical and Real-World groups and train complementary RL experts specialized for each reasoning group. For MOPD , we consolidate the capabilities of both experts into a unified generalist through multi-teacher on-policy distillation, where routed experts provide token-level supervision on student-induced trajectories. We investigate both forward- and reverse-KL objectives for capability integration. Extensive experiments demonstrate the effectiveness of MMVistaReason from three perspectives. (1)

# 2 Related Work 

## 2.1 Multimodal Reasoning Recipes 

Early multimodal training recipes primarily relied on LLM-generated visual instructions and super-vised fine-tuning over broad task mixtures, establishing general-purpose visual instruction-following capabilities [28, 24]. Subsequent studies introduced domain-specific or rationale-enriched supervision, including MathV360K, LLaVA-CoT-100K, and MAmmoTH-VL, to provide more explicit reasoning signals and improve performance in specialized scenarios [48, 65, 13]. More recent data-centric efforts further investigate the effects of context sources, data composition, reasoning quality, and difficulty-aware sample selection, shifting attention from data scaling toward deliberate curation of multimodal reasoning supervision [2, 26]. Beyond supervised fine-tuning, recent works have explored 3MMVistaReason: Toward Open-Data and Post-Training Recipes for Multimodal Reasoning 

reinforcement learning with outcome-level or step-level rewards for multimodal reasoning [17, 38, 74]. R1-Onevision and OpenMMReasoner further adopt two-stage SFT-RL pipelines, combining supervised initialization with subsequent policy optimization to improve multimodal reasoning performance [22, 68, 75]. However, existing open multimodal reasoning recipes typically optimize a single gen-eralist model over aggregated mixed-domain data, without explicitly addressing the heterogeneous capabilities required by different reasoning scenarios [26, 68, 75]. Meanwhile, controlled studies have shown that VLM fine-tuning can exhibit both positive and negative cross-task transfer, suggesting limitations of uniform joint optimization and motivating more adaptive training strategies [45]. Our work builds upon these efforts by combining systematic data construction, complementary expert specialization, and capability integration for reliable multimodal reasoning post-training. 

## 2.2 Knowledge Distillation 

Knowledge distillation transfers capabilities from a strong teacher to a compact student, traditionally by matching softened output distributions [14, 67]. For autoregressive models, sequence-level distillation further extends this paradigm by training students on complete teacher-generated sequences rather than independent token labels [19]. Recent LLM distillation methods explore objectives tailored for generative models, such as MiniLLM’s reverse-KL optimization [11]. In multimodal reasoning, offline distillation commonly leverages strong teachers to generate structured rationales or reasoning trajectories, which are then used as fixed supervised fine-tuning targets [65, 13, 15]. However, in practical settings, this paradigm introduces a mismatch between teacher-generated trajectories and student-induced states during autoregressive generation [1]. On-policy distillation addresses this issue by sampling trajectories from the student policy and query-ing teachers for token-level supervision on student-induced prefixes [1]. Recent studies further extend this paradigm through self-distillation, efficient prefix-level supervision, and relaxed imitation ob-jectives [77, 73, 20]. Beyond single-teacher transfer, MOPD integrates multiple domain-specific RL teachers into a unified student [36, 9, 69, 16, 51]. On-policy distillation has also been explored in multimodal models: VOLD combines GRPO with distillation from a text-only reasoning teacher, Visual-Advantage OPD emphasizes tokens containing fine-grained visual evidence, and OPOD co-ordinates specialized teachers across text, image, and audio modalities [3, 29, 78]. Building upon these advances, our work employs multi-teacher on-policy distillation to consolidate complementary real-world and analytical RL experts into unified multimodal generalists. 

# 3 MMVistaReason Recipes 

In this section, we present the MMVistaReason recipe framework, which consists of three components: Data Cleaning Recipe, Data Construction Recipe, Model Training and Integration Recipe. Together, they provide a unified pipeline from open-data curation to SFT and RL data construction, domain-specific RL training, and multi-teacher OPD integration.

After obtaining the complementary RL experts, we integrate their specialized capabilities back into the unified SFT model through multi-teacher on-policy distil-lation (MOPD) . The student is initialized from the SFT generalist and generates responses using its current policy. For expert routing, each training sample is assigned to the corresponding Real-World or Analytical expert according to its reasoning group. Rather than generating independent teacher trajectories, the selected expert evaluates the prefixes actually visited by the student and provides token-level distributional supervision on these on-policy states. Specifically, for each student prefix st = ( x, y<t), the routed expert πϕg provides its top-K token probabilities. The student probabilities are evaluated on the same teacher-selected token support, allowing the student to absorb the distributional preferences of the expert. We compare MVR-SFT-49K, MVR-SFT-124K, and MVR-SFT-355K, which are constructed from different teachers in the cascade, together with the full mixed dataset MVR-SFT-528K. At 4B, MOPD-RKL and MOPD-FKL reach 72.3 and 72.8 overall, while at 9B they reach 74.4 and 74.2, outperforming Mixed-RL. We further study the effect of KL direction in MOPD across model scales. Both Forward-KL and Reverse-KL outperform the SFT baseline and Mixed-RL in overall performance, confirming the effectiveness of expert distillation on student-induced on-policy trajectories. At 4B, Forward-KL outperforms Reverse-KL (72.8 vs. 72.3), whereas at 9B, Reverse-KL performs slightly better (74.4 vs. 74.2), revealing a scale-dependent preference for KL direction. Specifically, the two KL directions exhibit task- and scale-dependent preferences. At 4B, Reverse-KL performs better on several real-world benchmarks, while Forward-KL is stronger on most remaining tasks. At 9B, Reverse-KL becomes more competitive across benchmarks. This pattern suggests that its mode-seeking behavior may become more beneficial as student capacity increases, whereas smaller students benefit more from the broader distribution matching of Forward-KL. Since both RL experts are initialized from the same SFT generalist, their output distributions remain relatively aligned with the student, allowing both KL objectives to provide effective supervision. Forward-KL offers broader distribution matching that may be easier for smaller students to absorb, whereas Reverse-KL can better exploit sharper expert preferences at larger scale. Importantly, the gap between the two objectives remains small at both scales, suggesting that MOPD is robust to the exact KL direction and that expert-based on-policy integration is the primary source of improvement. 

# 5 Conclusion 

In this work, we present MMVistaReason , an open-data framework and adaptive training recipe for reliable multimodal reasoning. Rather than treating multimodal reasoning as a problem driven only by model scaling, we view it as a joint challenge of data construction, difficulty-aware sample selection, expert specialization, and capability integration. Through systematic data cleaning, answer normalization, model-aware difficulty filtering, cascaded rejection sampling, and Answer-Likelihood Best-of-N trajectory selection, MMVistaReason transforms heterogeneous open data into high-quality and verifiable reasoning supervision. Building on this corpus, we develop a training pipeline that establishes strong SFT generalists, then trains complementary RL experts for reasoning scenarios, and finally integrates their specialized capabilities through multi-teacher on-policy distillation. Extensive experiments across 4B and 9B scales show that MMVistaReason achieves strong and balanced per-formance across diverse multimodal reasoning benchmarks, while analyses reveal that supervision difficulty, trajectory quality, and integration behavior interact with student model capacity. In partic-ular, domain-specific specialization followed by MOPD better consolidates complementary expert capabilities with reduced cross-domain interference. Overall, MMVistaReason provides a practical and scalable path for building reliable multimodal reasoning models from open data. 16 MMVistaReason: Toward Open-Data and Post-Training Recipes for Multimodal Reasoning

# B Experimental Details 

## B.1 Distillation Details 

For cascaded reasoning trajectory distillation, we employ Qwen3.5-9B, Qwen3.5-27B, and Qwen3.5-122B-A10B as teacher models, deployed on 1, 2, and 4 NVIDIA A100 GPUs with 80 GB memory, respectively. To control the computational cost of large-scale trajectory generation, all teacher models are run in instruct mode ( enable thinking=False ). For each input sample, we generate four candidate trajectories and set the maximum generation length to 16,384 tokens. Under this deployment configu-ration, the 9B, 27B, and 122B-A10B teachers process approximately 200, 120, and 80 samples per hour, respectively, corresponding to roughly 800, 480, and 320 generated trajectories per hour. This cascaded design reduces distillation cost by routing only difficult samples to larger teachers.

## B.3 Evaluation Details 

We conduct evaluation using VLMEvalKit [7]. All evaluations are performed on NVIDIA H200 GPUs with 141GB memory. For evaluation metrics, we replace traditional exact string matching with CompassVerifier [30], which adopts an LLM-as-a-Judge to more reliably assess response correctness. Following OpenDataArena [4] and the official evaluation setting, we use deterministic decoding with temperature 0.0, top-p 1.0, and top-k -1. We further apply a repetition penalty of 1.05 and set the maximum response length to 16,384 tokens. Responses that do not produce a valid final answer within this budget are not counted as correct. For benchmarks with unusually large performance deviations, we additionally conduct manual inspections to verify the reliability of the evaluation results.
